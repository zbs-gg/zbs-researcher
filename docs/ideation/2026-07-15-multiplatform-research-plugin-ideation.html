Topic Context

Nikita already runs a private research skill (Reddit + AI deep-research, X via Grok, YouTube via Gemini, HN+comments, and — the crown jewel — Telegram channels parsed via Telethon on a separate account, "gold that doesn't exist on the external internet, especially in comments"). He wants to release it free once TikTok/Instagram can be added cheaply, and he wants every source reaction-weighted (upvotes/likes/views/comments), not just fetched. The existing base is the deep-research plugin v0.2.0 — Python stdlib-only connector registry, parallel threading, ERROR.md degrade, manifest.json, plan-first, HTML brief. Budget canon: no Anthropic/OpenAI API spend by default; flat-rate subs (Claude Max, Codex) and local Mac compute (64 GB, LM Studio/MLX) preferred over per-token APIs.

The hard facts that shaped these ideas (July 2026): Reddit self-service OAuth closed since Nov 2025 ("Responsible Builder Policy" — apply and wait days-to-never); Reddit .json dead (403 via TLS-fingerprint + IP-reputation since ~May 2026); RSS throttled to 1 req/min; Arctic Shift is the best free no-auth Reddit path (score fields plausible but unconfirmed). LinkedIn is the one genuinely hostile platform — Proxycurl was sued dead (July 2025), every automated path is banned/shallow/expensive. Local Whisper on his Mac = $0 transcription at 7-10× realtime; local Gemma-3 vision = $0/image. ScrapeCreators is genuinely pay-as-you-go (100 free credits, non-expiring), one vendor covering the three hardest gaps (Reddit comment trees, TikTok's 21 endpoints, IG reels/comments) behind a single key.

Two clarifications from Nikita during review (these reshaped the top ideas): (1) He installs this as a skill for clients too — so anything that depends on his 64 GB Mac (local Whisper/vision) can't be the default; portability beats personal-$0. (2) ~$10/month is an acceptable ceiling. A live pricing check then showed cloud transcription/vision is pennies — Groq Whisper-turbo $0.04/hr audio, Gemini Flash vision sub-cent/image, so $10/mo buys ~20-25× the realistic target volume. Net effect: the design now carries two deployment profiles — a self profile (local, $0, Mac-bound) and a client/portable profile (cloud-cheap, installs anywhere) — selected by config, not baked in. And for a client install, a cheap managed API beats a free-but-flaky OSS scraper the client can't babysit.

Ranked Ideas

1. $0-first, with a pay-per-use adapter — never a flat subscription

Axis: architecture-cost · source-access

Revised after a live pricing check (2026-07-15): the original "one $47/mo slot" was the wrong shape for low, uncertain volume — corrected to $0-first + metered fallback.

Description: Day one works with zero keys: Telegram (Telethon) + HN + Reddit via Arctic Shift + YouTube Data API (free 10k units/day) + yt-dlp→local Whisper + own Grok sub + Meta Ad Library (free) + local Gemma-3 vision, plus free-OSS scrapers where they hold up (instaloader for logged-out IG, davidteather/TikTok-Api for TikTok). The paid layer is a thin platform-adapter whose vendor is a config choice — free-oss → apify → scrapecreators — engaged only when a free path is throttled, and billed per use. The goal is a literal $0 in a quiet month, spend only on real overflow. The key correction: don't buy a monthly subscription. The genuinely metered option is ScrapeCreators — 100 free credits, and its $47 "Freelance" is not a monthly fee but 25,000 non-expiring credits (≈ 5-12 months at your volume, ~$4-9/mo amortized). Apify's Free plan gives $5/mo of credit (cheap actors: TikTok apidojo $0.30/1k) but hard-caps — overflow forces the $29/mo Starter, which overshoots low usage.

Basis: direct: "ScrapeCreators… 100% pay-as-you-go, no subscriptions, credits never expire; Freelance $47 = 25,000 credits @ $1.88/1k" + "Apify Free $5/mo credit, hard-caps; Starter $29/mo; apidojo TikTok $0.30/1k" + "Reddit's own API free 100 QPM for personal use (but Nov 2025 Responsible Builder Policy approval)" + "no Anthropic/OpenAI API by default; local Mac compute preferred." external: instaloader (MIT, active Apr 2026), davidteather/TikTok-Api (active, needs proxies) as the $0 flaky tier.

Rationale: This is the literal answer to "doesn't cost a spaceship," corrected for low, uncertain volume: a flat subscription is the worst shape — you pay in months you barely touch it. Metering means a quiet month is $0 and a busy month is a few dollars. Making the vendor a config choice (not a hardcoded key) also fits the public release and the two deployment profiles: on a client install a cheap managed API (ScrapeCreators/Apify per-use) is the right default because the client can't babysit proxies for a flaky OSS scraper; the free-OSS tier is the self profile where you accept fragility for $0.

Downsides: The $0 free-OSS tier is flaky by nature — TikTok-Api needs proxies and upkeep against bot-detection; instaloader risks account locks if run authenticated (safe only logged-out, light, delayed). So "$0" buys lower reliability; the metered adapter is the reliability upgrade, not a luxury. Reddit's free API needs the Nov-2025 approval (or stay on Arctic Shift). Load-bearing check: confirm Arctic Shift actually returns Reddit score fields before defaulting to it — reaction-weighting for Reddit hangs on it. The $10 ScrapeCreators "Solo Dev" tier (5k credits) is reported by third parties but not on the live pricing page — check the billing dashboard.

2. One media-to-text adapter with a pluggable backend — cloud by default, local as opt-in

Axis: architecture-cost · deployment-portability

Revised after "I install this on clients too": the local-only spine was wrong for a product shipped to other machines. A live pricing check (2026-07-15) showed cloud transcription is pennies, so portability wins with no real cost hit.

Description: YouTube, TikTok, IG Reels, IG carousels, LinkedIn media all need transcription or image-reading. Build it once: the connector contract shrinks to "return bytes + metadata," and everything routes through one media-to-text stage — but its backend is a deployment-profile config, not hardcoded. Client / portable profile (default): cloud STT + vision that runs on any machine — Groq Whisper-turbo at $0.04/hour audio and Gemini Flash vision at sub-cent/image. Self profile (opt-in): local MLX Whisper + Gemma-3 vision for your own $0 runs on the 64 GB Mac. Same interface, backend swapped by who's running it. Platform transcript endpoints (ScrapeCreators, ~$0.001-0.002/call, flat by length) stay as an optional shortcut where auto-caption quality is enough.

Basis: direct: "Groq Whisper-turbo $0.04/hr audio (5 hrs/mo = $0.20); Gemini 2.5 Flash vision $0.00008-0.00046/image; $10/mo ≈ 250 hrs audio or 20k images — target volume ~$0.40/mo, 3-4% of ceiling" + "MLX Whisper ≈7-10× realtime, $0 marginal but Apple-Silicon-bound" + "ScrapeCreators transcript flat ~$0.001-0.002/call, platform auto-caption quality."

Rationale: The biggest lever, corrected for the fact that this ships to clients: local compute is $0 for you but doesn't install on a client's ordinary laptop. Making the backend a profile choice keeps your personal $0 path and gives clients a portable one that costs cents — $10/mo buys ~20-25× the realistic target volume. Still one integration, not five.

Downsides: Two backends = two code paths to test (cloud + local), though the interface hides it. Cloud STT/vision means client data leaves the machine (a privacy note for client contracts). Gemini/Groq are per-token APIs — allowed here because they're neither Anthropic nor OpenAI and the spend is trivial, but it's still a key the client must hold.

Confidence: 90%  ·  Complexity: Medium

3. Telegram: a standing two-edge graph, with folders as auto-proposed views

Axis: telegram-graph

Description: Build one persistent Telegram graph with two edge types: subscriber-overlap (from channels.getChannelRecommendations, confirmed live & Telethon-callable, zero cost) for "who's adjacent," and forward/mention edges (mined via Telethon) for "who influences whom." Walk forwards backwards to find the upstream originator channels everyone in a niche forwards from — one hop upstream is the whole value for fast-moving niches. Then invert folder authority: the graph proposes folder membership per niche and Nik prunes a weekly diff ("+4 candidates, 2 gone quiet, 1 drifted"), with rule-based entry/exit thresholds so folders don't bloat monotonically.

Basis: direct: "channels.getChannelRecommendations CONFIRMED live, Telethon-callable 2026… recommends by SUBSCRIBER OVERLAP (not content)… BFS from seed channels = cheapest defensible graph expansion" + "Edge = source→dest per forwarded message = 'who influences whom', complements subscriber-overlap." external: maxbundscherer/telegram-analysis (forward graph, NetworkX+Louvain), MargotP/telegram_similar_channels_finder (working call), S&P/Russell reconstitution (rule-based membership refresh).

Rationale: Subscriber-overlap alone converges on the incestuous core of each niche (it recommends more-of-the-same by construction) — the forward layer is what surfaces the small high-signal channels that are the actual "gold." Making the folder a materialized graph view turns his stated "periodic refresh" chore into an approval queue, and it's the single most demo-able feature for a public release.

Downsides: Non-Premium accounts get a truncated recommendations list; the full graph likely needs Telegram Premium on the parsing account (a small monthly cost — verify the exact figure, it is not in the research). Forward-graph mining only sees public forwards; heavily-private niches leak less signal.

Confidence: 85%  ·  Complexity: Medium

4. Comments are the reaction; counters are the tiebreaker

Axis: reactions-ranking

Description: Redefine "reaction-weighted" away from raw likes/upvotes. The comment tree is the reaction; counters are its cheapest proxy. Run a local model over comment trees ($0 on his Mac) to score stance (agree / dispute / tried-it-and-failed / bought-it) and extract claims, then rank by evidenced reaction. Weight what's expensive to fake — replies-per-view, tree depth, distinct-commenter count, presence of disagreement — and actively penalize the like-heavy/comment-light signature of botted posts, emitting a "too clean to be real" flag as its own research output. Attach a collection-confidence flag (verified / estimated / partial-failure) to every count so a failed scrape never reads as "nobody reacted."

Basis: direct: "gold that doesn't exist on external internet, especially comments" + "IG comments ~90% success, most error-prone" + "Arctic Shift score fields plausible but unconfirmed" + "Mac 64GB + LM Studio/MLX local models available." reasoned: his target niches (OF-agency, infoproducts) are literally taught engagement-inflation on BlackHatWorld — a ranker that trusts platform counters imports its own subjects' manipulation into its output, so expensive-to-fake interaction structure is a directly-motivated design choice, not a generic one.

Rationale: This is the plugin's defensible differentiator — every aggregator ships like-counts; almost none ship "412 comments, 61% practitioners confirming, top dispute: chargebacks." And it's the one ranking decision that's painful to retrofit: build on raw counts first and you re-score the whole historical corpus later.

Downsides: Local LLM comment-scoring at corpus scale is the heaviest compute item here (still $0 in cash, but real wall-clock). Stance-scoring quality on multilingual RU/Asian creator-economy comments is unproven and needs its own eval on his data.

Confidence: 85%  ·  Complexity: Medium-High

5. Venue-normalized reaction index, calibrated by cross-posts

Axis: reactions-ranking

Description: A HN thread at 300 points and a TikTok at 50k views aren't comparable on raw numbers, so express every item's engagement relative to its own venue's trailing baseline, indexed to 100 (a "wRC+ for posts": 180 = 80% above normal for that subreddit/channel/hashtag), with small-sample items regressed toward the venue prior (the Wilson score — which is literally Reddit's own "best"-sort math). Then use cross-posted content — the same URL on HN and Reddit, the same clip on TikTok/Reels matched by local-Whisper transcript similarity — as paired observations to fit empirical "exchange rates" between platforms. Dedup, which the pipeline needs anyway, becomes the calibration instrument for free.

Basis: external: FanGraphs wRC+/OPS+ (100 = park-and-era-adjusted baseline); Evan Miller "How Not To Sort By Average Rating" (Wilson score, Reddit's real "best" sort); The Economist Big Mac Index (PPP via one cross-listed good). direct: local Whisper is $0 for the transcript-matching substrate, which the constraints already require building.

Rationale: Without venue-relative normalization the "reaction-weighted" promise is structurally broken — TikTok drowns everything on raw counts. This is the smallest algorithm that fixes it, and cross-post calibration is the only cross-platform bridge that's an own-eval on his own feed rather than trusting someone's benchmark.

Downsides: Needs a rolling per-venue history before the index means anything (cold-start problem — pairs with the corpus-daemon idea). Cross-post pairs may be sparse for niche RU/TG content that never touches HN/Reddit, weakening exchange-rate fits exactly where his gold lives.

Confidence: 80%  ·  Complexity: Medium

6. Reaction-gated two-stage pipeline with per-niche sentinels

Axis: architecture-cost

Description: Exploit the pricing asymmetry the platforms already encode: broad search is ~100× the cost of polling known IDs (YouTube search.list=100 units vs videos.list=1; TikTok search vs profile credits). Per niche, run one expensive broad sweep to elect ~20-50 "sentinel" accounts/hashtags/subreddits, then poll only sentinels on cheap endpoints. Split every pull into a cheap metadata pass (counts only) → reaction pre-score → and only then spend the expensive step (transcription, comment trees, paid credits) on the top-N survivors. A sentinel engagement spike is the tripwire that authorizes the next paid broad sweep.

Basis: direct: "commentThreads.list=10 units (hundreds/day fine), search.list=100 units (expensive), videos.list=1" + "ScrapeCreators 100 free credits, ~$0.0006-0.002/credit." external: WHO GISRS sentinel influenza surveillance (continuous cheap monitoring under a hard sampling budget, escalate on signal).

Rationale: This makes reaction-weighting pay for itself as cost control, not just a ranking feature — it turns the cost ceiling from a hope into a design property: steady-state spend = (sentinel count × cheap-endpoint price), with paid searches gated behind an observable trigger instead of a cron job.

Downsides: Sentinel election can miss a brand-new channel that hasn't accumulated signal yet (the tripwire is lagging). Some tension with a strict "ignore anything <48h" freshness rule — this idea needs early velocity as its trigger, so freshness policy must be decided coherently with it.

Confidence: 80%  ·  Complexity: Medium

7. A money-tier: treat spend/revenue as the highest-weight reaction

Axis: missed-sources

Description: Add a distinct source class whose "reaction" metric is money, not applause: Meta Ad Library (what OF-agencies & course-sellers pay to promote), Gumtrends (what infoproducts actually sell, est. revenue on 250k+ products), Whop (paid cohort/infoproduct data), Skool leaderboards (which paid communities are actually active), TikTok Creative Center top ads. Tag it separately from the reaction-weighted content lane, and let the divergence between the two tiers — what discourse says is hot vs what money confirms — be the research finding. Sustained ad-spend over weeks is the one engagement signal nobody bots against themselves.

Basis: direct: "Meta Ad Library API (official, free ~200 calls/hr; identity verification + App Review 5-10 days; full commercial detail EU-ONLY under DSA, US = bucketed/political) — top gap for OF-agency/infoproduct ad-spy" + "Gumtrends 250k+ products w/ est revenue — what actually SELLS" + "Whop API, Skool leaderboards." external: wastewater epidemiology (hard-to-fake aggregate behavioral signal beating self-report).

Rationale: This is the single highest signal-per-dollar addition and the direct answer to "what sources did I miss" — three of his four niches are saturated with performative "I made $30k with AI" discourse; upvotes measure applause, ad-spend and revenue measure conviction. A hypothesis-validation tool that only reads discourse validates hype, not demand. Meta Ad Library is the one missed source that covers two niches at once — but its App Review has a 5-10 day lead time, so it must be filed now, aimed at EU scope.

Downsides: Meta Ad Library's full commercial detail is EU-only under DSA — US coverage is bucketed/political and much weaker. Several money sources (Gumtrends, Skool) are scraper-based, not official APIs, so they carry their own fragility and small cost.

Confidence: 85%  ·  Complexity: Low-Medium
